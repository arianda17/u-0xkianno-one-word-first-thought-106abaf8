# Entry — One Word, First Thought

## Winning word

**palimpsest** — 10 characters.

## Rejected variants

1. **thing** — pure filler; generic to the point of carrying no image or feeling at all (judge criterion 5).
2. **twilight** — safe and expected; a word everyone already has, so it fails the surprise test (criterion 2).
3. **vivid** — abstract meta-word; calls up no concrete picture, reading as a description rather than a chosen word.
4. **petrichor** — vivid (the smell of rain on dry earth) but the expected pick among word-lovers; loses to a rarer word on surprise.

## Why the winner fits the judge criteria

palimpsest is a parchment scraped clean and written over, with the older text still showing underneath: one word that conjures layers, erasure and memory in ten letters. It is not a word most readers would guess first, so it scores on surprise. It is specific and sensory rather than abstract, calling up a clear image and feeling, so it scores on vividness. And it carries a spark of wit — the very word is itself layered, a text within a text, which rewards a second look. It feels chosen rather than generic; it is no filler word. Length alone does not count, and ten characters earns nothing on its own; the word earns its place by being surprising, vivid, witty and chosen all at once.
